# Twenty pickup orders before a build decision

Use this protocol for the first Gen 8 field study. No data has been collected and no participants have been recruited. The pilot tests whether we can observe a useful decision in time. It cannot establish a population failure rate, causal dollar savings or product-market fit.

## Who and where

Recruit ten unrelated households that normally use pickup weekly at the same retailer in the same area. A household can be one shopper. Include recurring dinners, a meaningful grocery cap, and at least one shared ingredient or promised lunch. Recruit before seeing their next order; do not require a prior bad substitution. Record invitations, eligibility, refusals and dropouts. Enroll two consecutive eligible orders, including uneventful and unverified orders. Do not solicit a purchase from this research cohort as a substitute for an independent price test.

## Before the store changes anything

1. Obtain consent for a redacted decision log and agree on deletion dates. Keep study ID, not name, address, loyalty ID, card, email, login, order barcode, unredacted screenshot or full unrelated shopping history. Use a redacted excerpt or participant transcription when sufficient.
2. Freeze the planned meals, number of eaters, any named exclusions or reserved portions, store/slot, scope of cash cap (dinner only or whole pickup order), essentials already needed outside dinner, and whether a second store/trip is acceptable. Never turn this research intake into proposed mandatory onboarding.
3. Lock the household’s strongest locally usable workflow for both trips, current paid subscriptions and setup state. Permit its usual retailer preferences, notes, saved lists and partner texts. Document whether a comparison uses an already-paid tool or a free one. Offer a realistic free planning alternative when relevant; do not artificially weaken or overburden the comparator.
4. The household uses its normal method. Record the original dated retailer preview, if available, without changing a live order for research. A prechecked cart, receipt, list of dinners and actual meals served are four separate records.

## During an eligible decision

Time-stamp the first actionable information, actual deadline or last feasible action, and which facts were visible to the shopper. Record the normal workflow’s proposed action and rationale *before* revealing the research answer. Independently give the researcher the same timestamped evidence, without the receipt or the normal answer. Return the research answer in shadow if the window is tight; never delay the shopper’s response. If shown in time, record the actual decision and whether advice changed it. The shopper chooses in the retailer. Researchers do not order or ask anyone to buy a filler item.

For a fee reversal, compare like with like: same store, slot, payment/benefit conditions and all unchanged groceries. Prefer the retailer’s displayed full totals for each version if safely available. Record qualifying subtotal, displayed fee, applied discount and later reward separately. Unknown eligibility → conditional answer. No second cart preview → do not claim an exact amount due. Never apply a pre-order threshold to a retailer-caused short fill without explicit evidence.

For a missing line, record the originally intended quantity, unit and meal deadline; whether the cart is a complete snapshot; what has been matched, received or refunded; and whether another adult already resolved it. An intentional deletion closes the need. An unexplained omission does not. “Unverified” is a valid answer; do not convert it to a purchase instruction. A sent note has a date/version and no automatic authority to overwrite either person's basket.

## At pickup and after dinner

Record final charges, verified refunds, top-up purchases needed to cover the original plan, outstanding credits and any extra trip. Keep ordered, billed, received, safe/acceptable to the household, feasible to serve and actually served distinct. Only ask the cook about the affected meal, and honor “unknown.” Measure both adults’ total active time, visits to each app, corrections and repeat questions. Separate research paperwork, researcher labor and waiting time from normal household work; record all three separately. Count initial product-equivalent setup on shop one. Never count a researcher’s fast answer as zero service cost.

## Blank order record

Copy once per eligible order. Leave missing fields null rather than guessing. Each line below can be a short phrase or a link to a consented, redacted local note.

- Household pseudonym / order number in sequence (1 or 2) / eligible week / comparator and baseline setup:
- Store and fulfillment mode / originally selected pickup date / whether another trip is acceptable:
- Original cap and scope / relevant dinner and servings / named lunch or exclusion / allowed alternatives this week:
- At-order and decision-time evidence timestamps / actual deadline / cart complete or excerpt / missing facts:
- Store’s before and after previews / eligible merchandise / service fee / charges still uncertain / later reward / did the price include the fee already:
- Original need and usable quantity / proposed substitute or omitted line / original dinner still possible, yes/no/unknown:
- Incumbent action, rationale and time recorded before reveal / blinded research answer and time / shown before cutoff, yes/no:

- Setup, shopper, cook, correction and researcher minutes separately / would family use the note again unprompted:

## How to interpret the twenty records

Keep the full denominator: all twenty scheduled orders, including routine shops, attrition and missing verification. Report observed, paired shadow and hypothetical results separately. Only one path is actually purchased; the alternative is not a second observed household outcome. Never attribute the full fee as a saving when the family's actual alternative already avoided it. Do not infer time saved by subtracting researcher time from an observed participant's time. A fair convenience comparison needs counterbalanced, matched tasks or a later independent cohort and timing for both sides.

Classify stage separately from result. Stage: ordinary shop/no decision; pre-submission; actionable offer; pickup; or afterward. Result against the locked comparator: better, same, worse or unknown. Specify if both methods failed. Record verification as confirmed, contradicted or missing, and distinguish an observed outcome from a projected comparison. Tag harm and extra work independently; a changed decision does not automatically mean a positive outcome. Review any false claim of budget or meal coverage, missed deadline, duplicate purchase, unwanted swap or surprise additional trip first. A single false assurance blocks that promise until fixed and retested on unseen examples.

**Proposed advancement rule:** two independently documented, verifiable incremental decisions in different households justify a larger study of the relevant stage only. This is a practical resource gate, not statistical significance. If the benefit is time only, use a separate matched timing study; if late only, direct the next study to recovery. If facts routinely cannot be obtained within the cutoff, remove the live promise. If evidence is unverified, improve measurement before repeating. Passing this screen does not authorize a native build or annual offer.

For repeatability use Gen 8’s 100 consecutive orders for incidence, separate 30-exception decision comparison, eight-week outcome or preregistered convenience endpoint, then independent paid/retention gates. Link every proposed change to a failure in the log. Design response options with people who use VoiceOver, large text or one hand before claiming those flows work.
