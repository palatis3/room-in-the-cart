# Dedicated-owner Default QoS: assessment only

A dedicated speech owner at Default QoS is a legitimate scheduling-design hypothesis, not automatically warning suppression. It is not established as safe or sufficient by the retained evidence, and no implementation change was made.

Apple's current Thread Performance Checker guidance explicitly recommends keeping a waiting thread's QoS at or below its signaler's when the dependency cannot be avoided through a synchronous variant, while preferring removal of unnecessary synchronous emulation. This gives a technical basis for matching an isolated waiter to its dependency rather than assigning it greater scheduling importance that its blocking dependency cannot fulfill. [Apple: Diagnosing performance issues early](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/diagnosing-performance-issues-early).

QoS still describes the importance and responsiveness requirements of the work. Apple's archived guide classifies actions requiring prompt results as User-initiated and calls Default a special class rather than a normal developer work category. That is a reason to justify this owner as an adapter to the observed framework service, not to infer that all user-triggered speech work belongs at Default. [Apple: Prioritize Work with Quality of Service Classes](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Performance/Conceptual/EnergyGuide-iOS/PrioritizeWorkWithQoS.html).

Three effects must be separated:

1. **Direct UI-thread isolation is a substantive benefit.** The r4/r5 architecture submits commands and receives acknowledgments asynchronously; native Pause executes on its owner. Earlier exact r4 stacks demonstrate the formerly direct MainActor Pause wait moved to that owner. No main-thread join or synchronous wait for native completion is added. This structural benefit exists before changing owner QoS. UI acknowledgment alone is not physical Pause or silence.
2. **Default may correctly remove the owner priority mismatch, while retaining its wait.** R4's owner Start getter/Pause/Stop getter records report User-initiated waiting on Default. At an actually effective Default owner QoS, that particular mismatch may cease. Native calls, their serialization, cancellation generation checks, failed-Stop barrier, and terminal retirement rules can remain identical. This would neither shorten the synchronous native wait by itself nor make Stop preempt a native call already blocking the sole owner. Lower scheduling preference could delay admission or resumption under contention; no retained measurement bounds that effect.
3. **Framework-main waits remain a separate defect.** R4 already recorded TextToSpeech work dispatched to main after isolation. This hosted original run contains four such records as well as direct Pause. Changing the owner property does not change those observed main-thread waiters' QoS, remove their synchronous work, or establish a remedy. Framework behavior could vary with creation context, but there is no evidence here that it will stop dispatching those callbacks to main.

The fault messages establish the dependency's observed QoS at those events, not a stable AVFoundation policy, complete logical signaler identity, or effective QoS throughout all commands and supported OS versions. A source property of `.default` would require runtime confirmation; it is not itself proof of the effective scheduling class or complete dependency graph.

To treat a later Default-owner variant as a responsiveness change rather than a warning-count change, review must retain the original async command/acknowledgment and cancellation semantics, establish prompt real Play/Pause/Resume/Stop behavior (especially Stop after a pending command, under realistic contention), and distinguish logical UI state from actual speech termination. Existing model tests can protect state ordering but do not measure those physical or timing properties. An owner warning disappearing while its blocking duration remains would prove only mismatch removal. Main-framework warnings and missing diagnostic attachments must still be reported; acceptance requires the unchanged complete strict gate and original UI journeys.

**Decision:** no automatic promotion of unchanged r5, and no QoS patch justified as the overall fix. A separately scoped Default-owner candidate could be reasonable after root review because it follows documented dependency alignment, but it remains an experiment needing responsiveness evidence. It cannot substitute for root's current retained-trace investigation of the framework-main waiter. No checker or warning inventory exception is warranted.
